Carrera · 7 October 2026 ::toc{depth=2} :::macros \gdef\R{\mathbb{R}} \DeclareMathOperator{\im}{im} \DeclareMathOperator{\rank}{rank} \DeclareMathOperator{\nullity}{null} ::: Throughout, $T: V \to W$ is a linear map between vector spaces over $\R$. ## Definitions {#defs} :::definition[Kernel]{#def-kernel} The kernel of $T$ is $\ker T = \{\, v \in V : Tv = 0 \,\}$, the vectors $T$ sends to zero. ::: :::definition[Image]{#def-image} The image of $T$ is $\im T = \{\, Tv : v \in V \,\}$, the vectors $T$ reaches. ::: Both are subspaces.[^subspace] [^subspace]: Closed under addition and scaling: if $Tu = Tv = 0$ then $T(u + \lambda v) = 0$ for every scalar $\lambda$, and $Tu + \lambda Tv = T(u + \lambda v)$ is in the image. :::definition{#def-rank} The :dfn[rank] of $T$ is $\rank T = \dim \im T$, and its :dfn[nullity] is $\nullity T = \dim \ker T$. ::: For example, $T(x, y, z) = (x + y + z, 0, 0)$ sends the plane $x + y + z = 0$ to zero and the rest of $\R^3$ onto the first axis. Its kernel is that plane and its image that line (@fig-kernel), so $\rank T = 1$ and $\nullity T = 2$. ## Two theorems {#theorems} :::theorem[Rank–nullity]{#thm-rank-nullity} If $V$ is finite-dimensional then $$ \dim V = \dim \ker T + \dim \im T. $$ {#eq-rank-nullity} ::: :::proof Pick a basis $k_1, \dots, k_m$ of $\ker T$ and extend it to a basis $k_1, \dots, k_m, v_1, \dots, v_r$ of $V$. The vectors $Tv_1, \dots, Tv_r$ span $\im T$, and they are independent: if $$ \sum_{j=1}^{r} a_j\, Tv_j = 0 $$ then $\sum_j a_j v_j \in \ker T$, so every $a_j = 0$. Counting gives @eq-rank-nullity. ::: $T$ is :definition[:dfn[injective] when different vectors have different images]{#def-injective}, and :definition[:dfn[surjective] when its image is all of $W$]{#def-surjective}. The kernel decides the first: :::lemma[Injectivity test]{#lem-inj} $T$ is injective exactly when $\ker T = \{0\}$. ::: :::theorem[Dimension and bijectivity]{#thm-bij} Let $\dim V = \dim W < \infty$. Then $T$ is injective if and only if it is surjective. ::: :::proof By @lem-inj, the @lem-inj{ref=title}, $T$ is injective iff $\dim \ker T = 0$. By @thm-rank-nullity that holds iff $\dim \im T = \dim V = \dim W$, iff $T$ is surjective. Equation @eq-rank-nullity does all the work; compare @eq-bound, which only bounds the rank: $$ \rank T \le \min(\dim V, \dim W). $$ {#eq-bound} :::/proof :::figure[The kernel (plane) and image (line) of $T(x, y, z) = (x + y + z, 0, 0)$.]{#fig-kernel} ![A plane through the origin, and a line through it, in 3-space](img/kernel-image.svg){width=60%} ::: As @fig-kernel shows, @thm-rank-nullity says the two pictures add up to three dimensions. ## Revision {#revision} :::recall[When is a square matrix invertible?] When its kernel is trivial: by @lem-inj it is then injective, and by @thm-bij surjective too. Equivalently, its determinant is nonzero. ::: :::recall[A linear map $\R^5 \to \R^3$ is surjective. What is its nullity?] $2$. Its rank is $3$, and @thm-rank-nullity gives $5 = \nullity T + 3$. :::
