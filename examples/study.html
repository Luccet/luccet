Part of [Luccet's documentation](../index.html), whose look it wears through `../docs/docs.css`. ::: {.byline} [Lecture 7](lecture.html), 7 October 2026 · reviewed 9 October ## Kernel and image {#kernel-image} :::cornell{cols="1 2"} What does $T$ send to zero? +++ Its [kernel](lecture.html#def-kernel), $\ker T$, a subspace of $V$. It always holds $0$, so it is never empty. +++ What does $T$ reach? +++ Its [image](lecture.html#def-image), $\im T$, a subspace of $W$. +++ How do I show that $T$ is injective? +++ Show that $\ker T = \{0\}$ ([the injectivity test](lecture.html#lem-inj)): one equation, $Tv = 0$, instead of $Tu = Tv$ for every pair. ::: ## Rank–nullity {#rank-nullity} :::cornell{cols="1 2"} What does it say? +++ $\dim V = \dim \ker T + \dim \im T$ ([rank–nullity](lecture.html#thm-rank-nullity)): the nullity and the rank add up to the dimension of $V$. +++ Why is it true? +++ Extend a basis of $\ker T$ to one of $V$. The images of the vectors added are a basis of $\im T$. +++ An example? +++ $T(x, y, z) = (x + y + z, 0, 0)$: the kernel is the plane $x + y + z = 0$, the image the first axis, and $2 + 1 = 3$. +++ Why does it matter? +++ When $\dim V = \dim W$, injective and surjective are the same thing ([theorem 3](lecture.html#thm-bij)): a trivial kernel is enough for $T$ to be invertible. ::: :::summary[Summary] The kernel is what $T$ loses and the image what it reaches. Their dimensions, the nullity and the rank, add up to $\dim V$, so between spaces of the same dimension a trivial kernel is all it takes to be invertible. ::: ## Check yourself {#check} :::recall[A map $\R^4 \to \R^4$ has a kernel of dimension $1$. Is it surjective?] No. Its rank is $4 - 1 = 3$: its image is a 3-dimensional subspace of $\R^4$. ::: :::recall[Can a linear map $\R^2 \to \R^3$ be surjective?] No. Its rank is at most $\dim \R^2 = 2$. ::: :::recall[Can a linear map $\R^3 \to \R^2$ be injective?] No. Its nullity is at least $3 - 2 = 1$. ::: ## To do {#todo} - [x] Write the definitions in my own words - [ ] Prove rank–nullity without the notes - [ ] Exercises 7.1 to 7.4
